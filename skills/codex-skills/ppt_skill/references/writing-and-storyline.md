# Writing And Storyline

## Objective

Write like an internal expert explaining evidence and implications to busy technical managers. The report must help the audience understand what changed, why it matters, how it works, and what should be examined next.

## Story Construction

1. Define one management question for the deck.
2. Build one main storyline that answers it progressively.
3. Give every chapter a clear role in that storyline.
4. Write slide titles in sequence before writing body copy. Reading titles alone must reveal the complete argument.
5. Use bridge logic between slides: the output of one page becomes the starting point of the next.

Do not create multiple parallel frameworks merely to organize available material. Cases, APIs, technical layers, and opportunity points should serve the main storyline.

## Slide Writing Contract

Each content slide contains:

- one conclusion-style title;
- one core judgment;
- one primary case, evidence item, or explanatory visual;
- no more than three support groups;
- one concise bottom conclusion when the chosen template includes it.

The title must say what changed or what the evidence means. Avoid neutral labels such as “Background”, “Core Capabilities”, “Task Entry”, or “Opportunity”.

## Language Rules

- Open with the finding. Do not explain that the text is about to summarize, compare, remind, or conclude.
- Prefer subjects and actions: “App Intents exposes typed actions to the system.”
- Explain the capability before listing representative APIs.
- Use short paragraphs with varied sentence length. Split long chains of clauses.
- Use concrete mechanisms, numbers, cases, and boundaries instead of adjectives.
- Use ordinary language for management conclusions; keep API names as evidence.
- Keep vendor facts, developer observations, report judgments, and recommendations distinct.

## Prohibited Patterns

Do not use:

- “不是 X，而是 Y” or “不仅 X，更是 Y”;
- “从 X 到 Y” as a slogan;
- “重新定义”, “全新范式”, “关键一跃”, “底层逻辑”, “未来已来”;
- “值得注意的是”, “可以概括为”, “下面来看”, “总而言之”;
- empty phrases such as “全面提升”, “显著价值”, “形成闭环”, “深度赋能”;
- artificial counting frameworks unless the evidence naturally has that number of parts.

## Evidence Discipline

- Every factual claim that matters to the conclusion must map to a source ID.
- Official screenshots demonstrate only what is visible in the source.
- Generated illustrations explain relationships; they do not establish vendor facts.
- Do not enlarge one demo into an ecosystem-wide claim.
- Preserve time, beta status, supported scope, and material limitations when they affect the conclusion.

## Density Editing

Remove a sentence when deleting it does not reduce information. Replace repeated explanations with a diagram, matrix, or one stronger sentence. Small gray labels such as “official demo”, “source”, or “management takeaway” do not count as useful density unless they prevent a real ambiguity.
